## The doorway

- NFC is down the right side on both boards now (display `corner_layout`), so the top row is clock, battery, GPS on the left and cellular, WiFi, Bluetooth on the right, and the spot by the camera is open on both.
- **The doorway** is the camera hole's centre (`ferros_sys::cutout_hole`) as one more point in the dash's nearest-centre zones: the pixels of the top nearer it than any console circle's centre (owner, 2026-11-11: "that should be a point and a dist from BT and GPS"; a disc about the hole out to the nearest console circle until then). Nothing in it is a fixed pixel: the hole is measured glass, the corners are solved from the glass.
- A still tap in the doorway opens the row, or shuts it.
- A drag down that starts in the doorway pulls it; nothing else does (owner, 2026-11-11: "the second console row should only show if drug down from the area in the invisible camera circle"). Until then a drag down from anywhere on the top, a radio circle included, pulled it.
- In full screen there is no doorway: the console's rows are the app's (WINDOWS.md "The helm").

## Where the row sits: circle fitting inside out

The corners' circles are each tangent to the glass, the app's circle (from outside) and their neighbour. The quick row is the same fit turned inside out: seven circles inside the app's circle (six until full screen joined them, 2026-11-11), each tangent to it from inside, to a horizontal floor from above and to its neighbour, and the outer pair tangent to the glass's left and right edges (display quick.rs `layout`).

Owner, 2026-11-11, after trying the first cut on the Pro: "Good idea on the swipe down console! Let's make the circles always touch the left/right sides so move the bottom horizontal guide down until they scale up and touch." Until then the floor was the console's last row (the first row where the app circle's chord is the whole width) and the row stopped well short of the sides (husky r 81, 72, 58).

- **The solve.** Two unknowns: the floor, and the split, where the middle circle is centred (`middle`: tangent to the arc and the floor; each side's chain starts tangent to it). Until the seventh circle the split was the line the middle pair both touched. For a floor, the split moves until both outer circles are as near their walls; then the floor moves down until that nearness is nought. Both by bisection, the circles' centres on the closed-form line where the arc's room equals the floor's. Once per screen size, logged with its time (`display: quick row (x,y,r) … floor F, walls L/R hundredths, solved in N us`).
- **The walls are the glass, not the screen's edge**: measured as `corner_layout` measures them (`GlassOutline`, hairline and rim off). The glass's corners are not alike (the big one top left), so the split is not the centre: a pixel right of it on husky, six on akita, whose fat corner is rounder.
- **Collisions.** The console's corner circles (clock top left, cellular, WiFi, Bluetooth and NFC top right) are outside the app's circle and these inside it, both tangent to it, so they cannot overlap; their drawn rims (two and a half pixels) can meet, and the console's region redraw composes both. The camera hole is above the app's circle, and the middle circle, centred under it, meets the app circle's top where the hole touches it from outside (a pixel apart on both boards); the compose clips the row to the app's circle, so none of its rim reaches the hole.

Left to right: radios off, sound, brightness, full screen, torch, do not disturb, screencap. Full screen is the middle, largest circle (owner, 2026-11-11: full screen moves from the deck swipe "to a quick-row circle"); the two rings (brightness, torch) are the pair either side of it.

## Drawn once, then slid (2026-11-11)

The first cut grew the row out of the point under the camera, every circle drawn again at a new size each frame. On the Pro the owner found it "stupidly slow and jerky"; a console draw had been measured at 9.5 ms ("display: console: 32 draws, 9530 us each"), over a 120 Hz frame's 8.3, and the growing row drew six more circles (vessels, rings, words at a new size) every frame. Then: "Simply appearing and disappearing is fine too. no need for a scale in/out animation", and, refining it, "track the finger down from top tho and slide it in so it doesn't need a re-render, just a move and composite, then do the iterate/2 each frame, like we do for the settings page until it snaps in or out of place". The C∞ grow-and-fan is gone by the owner's word.

- **Drawn once.** Each circle is drawn into its own sprite at its size, only when its look changes (a tap, a ring turned, the sound mode, the language; display quick.rs `update`, `render`): its ring, its word, then its vessel under them, front to back, then decoded to working values in place. A sprite holds only the circle's disc out to its reach, row by row (spans): about 264 thousand pixels, 2.1 MiB, on husky; 1.4 MiB on akita.
- **Then slid.** The row's offset, whole pixels: 0 hidden, the slide's length open (husky 299 px: from the row's lowest row to the top of the app's circle, so hidden every pixel of it is above that circle). Each pass the console's worker composes the sprites at the offset under the console's own circles into the bars' decoded rows (`compose`, from `bar_decode`), clipped to the app's circle with exact coverage on the two edge columns, so the row comes out from behind the top of that circle and the console stays in front. Only the band the row covered and covers is composed; the render thread publishes what changed.
- **A step's work** (2026-11-11, owner on the Pro: "no reason we can't hit 120fps with a sliding console row"; his principle, "draw once, move the pixels"). On the worker: the console's rows copied from its decoded layer (main.rs `CON_DEC`, decoded once a console draw, never in a step), the sprites composed at the offset, and the rectangle that changed known from the bars' spans and the hit map's owners, no pixel compared. On the render thread: each row of that rectangle composed (the bars over the scene) and worked out to its codes once (`neon::codes_app`), then only dithered and packed into each of the four phases (`neon::dither_codes`). No geometry a pixel, and no drawing.
- **Why not the panel's words, moved.** The dither is per screen pixel: a phase's threshold at (x, y) is Bayer[y mod 4][x mod 4] × 4 + rank[(phase + x/4 + y/4) mod 4], so a band moved by a whole multiple of four rows is phase k's words exactly where it was phase k + rows/4's, and any other move needs the dither again (16 copies of the band, every phase at every row mod 4, 13 to 17 MiB, would cover them all). But the vessel's body is opaque but for 2⁻⁴ of what is behind it (orb `Vessel::at`), and the ODT is not linear, so no pixel of the row has words of its own: they depend on the scene behind, which differs at every offset. The codes are worked out once a step instead of once a phase, which is the part the moved band would have saved.
- **Not a hardware plane.** DECON's windows could carry the band and move it by a register write, but they blend Porter-Duff over (COMPOSITOR.md "Not used": they serve only pure selection), the app circle's clip moves across the band, and its phases would need their own buffers flipped in step: a design for the owner first, not built.
- **Memory.** The console's circles are drawn over the chrome rows alone again (`BAR`, husky 288 rows); the decoded rows (`BAR_W`, its back buffer, its spans) reach the row's floor (`BAR_TOP`, quick.rs `rows`: 417 on husky, 341 on akita). Against the wide row's first cut (the drawing buffer grown too): 1.4 MiB less there, 2.1 MiB of sprites: about 0.7 MiB more on husky, about 30.4 of the display's 32 MiB heap, which is why the heap grows on demand since that night (COMPOSITOR.md "Memory": the boot line `display: heap at start …` says what it is).
- **Measured**, once a slide lands open or hidden (main.rs `slide_shown`), three lines:
  - `display: slide: open: S steps sent, N shown in F frame starts; on the next frame A, one late B, later C`: the moves op 56 made, the ones that reached the glass (a step the map sent again before the worker took it is not shown), the frames from the first move's frame to the one showing the last, and how many frames after its move each step was shown (the map moves the row as a frame starts, so the next frame is on time). At the panel's rate: S = N = F = A, B = C = 0.
  - `display: slide: worker us mean/worst: circles …, sprites …, compose …, rect …, pass …`: the console's circles looked at, the row's sprites drawn (a look that changed), the rows copied and composed, the changed rectangle, the worker's whole pass.
  - `display: slide: publish us mean/worst …, move to arm …; rows composed …, published …; sprites drawn N`: the bars' publish on the render thread, op 56's move to the frame armed, the rows the worker composed and the rows published.
  - Before 2026-11-11's step (the Pro, 32 passes over several slides): circles 0.13 ms, sprites 0.64, compose 2.85, diff 1.40, a worker pass 5.0 ms (worst 10.0) and the publish 5.1 ms (worst 13.3), 315 rows (worst 387): a step took two frames and often three. A host stand-in of the new step (x86 scalar, husky's geometry, its ODT a scalar port; ratios, not the device's times) puts the worker at 2.2 times less and the publish at 2.4 times less.

## The slide and its stepping

- **The offset** follows the finger one to one from where the drag took hold (the doorway, the top's ground, a radio circle dragged down, a ring dragged up, or a switch dragged up or down: "Quick pages" below), between hidden and open.
- **The lift**: from hidden, the row goes open if it was pulled past a third of the way, else hidden; from open, hidden if pushed back past a third, else open. Each frame the panel showed, what is left to go halves (a right shift, the half pixel dropped), so it lands in a few frames and holds still (map quick.rs `step`). A still tap in the doorway sends it open or hidden the same way.
- **Frame timer on shown frames**: one step for each frame the panel showed (display op 24's frame-start count), never on a clock, so a missed render steps twice rather than slowing. The display arms a frame as soon as the bars it swaps in changed, so each step is on the glass at the next frame (2026-11-11: until then the change waited for the 10 Hz flips and the slide showed at 10 frames a second on the Pro, its steps waiting on those frames). Measured there before the fix: a step's work 5.4 ms mean (circles 0.09, sprites 1.2, compose 2.8, diff 1.3) and the bars' publish 2.4 ms, worst 12.3 and 14.2.
- **What held steps back** (2026-11-11, later; owner: "It looks WAY less than that. is it a 10-120hz flip issue?", then "I thought finger down triggered the 120..."). Op 24 returns as each armed frame starts (the frame-start interrupt masks the trigger again, so a frame is never sent twice), or after a hundred-and-twentieth of a second with none in flight, and the map steps only on a new count: a step's frame is the one the bars arm. The arm did fire for slide steps. What held them:
  - The render thread slept on the ring's tail, which the console's worker never moves: bars finished while a pass ran (a 10 Hz flip's present holds a pass up to a frame) waited for the next deadline, the flip a tenth of a second on, and the map, seeing no frame start, stood still meanwhile. Now every waker rings the render thread's doorbell (`BELL`).
  - A flip could land between steps and hold the render thread on its present; the bars now always go out armed, and the 10 Hz flip waits a tenth of a second after the last bars armed.
  - Op 56 never counted as an interaction (camera ops do), so a stale phase's refill (a whole phase in a pass, before the arm) could run between steps; a step holds it off now.
  - The worker dropped a move that came while its last result waited to be swapped in (it was nudged before the swap), showing it a step late, two halvings at once; it is nudged after the swap now and makes the next step while the render thread publishes the last.
  - A step's work (about 10 ms) was over a frame, so even on time a step showed every second or third frame.
- **Shutting**: push it back up, a still tap in the doorway, or a touch anywhere behind the row (that touch goes no further).
- **Taps on the row** count only once it is all the way open; while it slides, a finger on it is the slide's.

## The circles

Two kinds (2026-11-12, "Quick pages" below): a **level** (the two rings) opens its page on a tap and is set by a drag down; a **switch** (every other circle) acts on a tap, and a drag on it moves the row.

- **Brightness** (a level, a ring): a tap opens Appearance, the look's one page; a drag down sets preference 3 (32nds of a stop, 0 the brightest, 255 the dimmest), the dimmest the panel goes at the circle's bottom edge to the brightest at the screen's middle ("Quick pages" below; until 2026-11-11 a finger turned the ring about its centre, a whole turn the whole range, then until 2026-11-12 a pull counted from where it took hold opened Appearance past the bright end). Applied live while the finger drags (`set_pref_live`) and kept once at the lift (`set_pref`), as the Appearance slider does. The ring's lit share is how bright it is; while a finger drags it, the display draws the exact share the map sent into that circle's sprite again (the one circle that changed), so the lit end moves smoothly between the preference's steps.
- **Torch** (a level, a ring): a tap opens its page, where its on lights it at the level it was last set to (preference 42; the whole dimmer at first) with the strobe, lit share and timeout set there (until 2026-11-12 the tap did that); a drag down sets the level, 0 (off) at the circle's bottom edge to 255 at the screen's middle on the torch's own perceptual dimmer (docs/TORCH.md), and the level it was left at (if not off) is the one its on lights it at next. The map calls the `torch` endpoint waiting (`call`, not `call_live`: the kernel starts the torch process on demand and a live call would be refused while it starts), from a thread of its own (map quick.rs `worker`), so the main loop never waits on it. The torch's level is asked once each time the row starts to show, until it has answered and while it was last lit (its timeout may have put it out since). A torch that does not answer (no capsule stored, an older kernel with no grant) draws dimmed, and a tap or a drag does nothing.
- **Radios off** (a switch): every radio off at once, the set that was off before kept (preference 41, `radios_before`); a tap from all off puts that set back (all on when it was every radio). A drag moves the row (until 2026-11-12 a drag down opened Connections: no drag opens a page now).
- **Sound** (a switch): a tap goes round sound, vibrate, silent (preference 39, a device setting). Its word is the mode now. The audio service (AUDIO.md "Mixing") plays alert streams only in sound, media in every mode; nothing buzzes by it yet.
- **Do not disturb** (a switch): the person's, not the device's (SETTINGS.md). A tap asks the identity service (fgtw op 24) from the map's worker thread; the service puts it in the person's space, hands it to their fleet and fgtw.org, and writes it to the kernel (preference 40), which the circle shows. Before the identity service's first pull it is refused and the circle does not change. The audio service silences alert streams while it is on (AUDIO.md "Mixing").
- **Screencap** (a switch): the row shuts first; when it has landed the map sends op 56 with the capture flag, and the display takes the capture (as op 30 does) the first pass the bars on the screen hold none of the row, so the row is not in the picture. There is no clipboard yet (owner, 2026-11-07: "when we get the clipboard running that's where the screencap will live"), so the frame stays in the free surface op 30 wrote it to and its address goes to the console (`display: quick row screencap (no clipboard yet): G#…`).
- **Full screen** (a switch, the middle circle): a tap switches the app in front full screen or back (display op 43, the app window's own flag, kept while it is behind). Dimmed with no app in front, where a tap does nothing. A tap that turns full screen on shuts the row at once, no slide (map quick.rs `shut`): in full screen the console and the row are gone (owner, 2026-11-11, on the Pro: "helm should be functional but not visible in full screen, whilist console should be gone gone"), drawn nowhere and on no pixel of the hit map, the doorway with them, so nothing pulls the row and those pixels are the app's. Home's swipe inward switches full screen too, the first way since 2026-11-11 and the only way back (WINDOWS.md "The helm"; owner: "the swipe in on home whilist in a kist needs to show/hide controls"): the helm's wedges stay the system's on the hit map in full screen, undrawn, so the swipe that hid the controls brings them back. Its own light (lit while full screen) is never seen now. Until then the console's rows showed in full screen while the bars held some of the row, so a pull from the doorway brought the console and the row back over the app and this circle switched it off.
- Rotation lock: none (no rotation today).

Each circle's word is ferros_lang's, in the person's language, a word a line. A circle is lit while what it switches is on; a finger on it lifts it to the live ink's light.

### The drag down from a level

Where it starts: on a ring while the row stands all the way open (the hit map gives a quick circle's id, 32 + k, only then, and op 41 answers the circle's disc after it: x, y, r as the display's `quick::layout` draws it). The finger is held (the circle lightens) and nothing moves until it passes the slop (map `SLOP`, as every drag). Which way it goes is decided at the slop and kept for the rest of the touch: down (more down than across) is the level's; up moves the row; across does nothing (the row does not turn sideways).

Where the finger is, not how far it went:
- **Above the circle's bottom edge** (still on the circle, or anywhere above its lowest row y + r): nothing changes, and the ring shows what it was.
- **Once below it**, the level is the finger's place between two rows: 0 at the circle's bottom edge (the torch off; brightness the dimmest the panel goes, preference 3 at 255) and the whole level at the screen's vertical middle h/2 (the torch at 255; brightness the brightest, preference 3 at 0). It follows the finger both ways from then on: back up past the bottom edge is 0 again, down past the middle is the whole level.
- **The ease** (map quick.rs `level_at`): the ring's own ease, `ease` (the value itself thru the middle, the ramp's two corners smoothed across an eighth either side of each end, exactly 0 below and exactly 1 above), stretched so its exact 0 lands on the bottom edge and its exact 1 on the middle: share = ease(−1/8 + d · 5/4), with d the finger's share of the way from the bottom edge to the middle. Every derivative is 0 at both ends, so the level leaves 0 and reaches the whole with no kink and stops there without a step; between a fifth and four fifths of the way it is linear (slope 5/4). No clamp: the ends are the ease's own.
- **The ease made monotone** (2026-11-12): a corner was p times the C∞ bump step, whose slope rose to about twice the middle's and which dipped 0.65 % below 0 and rose 0.6 % past 1. A pull counted from where it took hold never showed it; a level at the finger's place would have, as a dead tenth of the run at the circle's edge where the byte held at 0 and a turn back near the middle. Now a corner is the bump step integrated (`bump_ramp`, Simpson's rule over sixteen slices): its slope is the bump step itself, rising 0 to 1 and never past, so the share never leaves [0, 1], never turns back, and is never steeper than the middle (measured on the host at a quarter pixel, both boards). The cost of the smooth start: the torch's first byte level comes 67 px below the edge on husky (53 on akita), and its last 68 px above the middle (54); `EASE_HALF` (an eighth, one shift) narrows both.
- **Each board's geometry** (the quick row's table above; the span 2wh/(w + h)): husky (1344 × 2992, span 1854): both rings' bottom edges at row 383, about 0.21 of the span; the middle at 1496; the run 1113 px, 0.60 of the span, the eased ends about 223 px each. akita (1080 × 2400, span 1489): bottom edges at 313, 0.21 of the span; the middle at 1200; the run 887 px, 0.60 of the span, the eased ends about 177 px each. Both rows are measured, not chosen: the circle is the solve's, the middle is the glass's.
- **What the level does**: brightness is applied live (`set_pref_live`, the panel follows the finger) and kept at the lift (`set_pref`); the torch is asked each new level from the map's worker thread (its endpoint, op 0), 0 putting it out, and at the lift its level, unless off, is kept as the one its page's on lights it at (preference 42; dragged to off, the kept level stays what it was). The ring draws the share the map sends (op 56's ring shares), so the lit arc moves with the finger: the visible level while dragging, nothing new for the display.
- **The torch's other settings**: a drag that lights it lights it as it is set: the strobe, the lit share and the timeout from its page (preferences 43-45), which the torch reads for itself at every request (docs/TORCH.md "Settings"); with the strobe off it is a plain light. Kept so because the drag is only the level, and a person who set a strobe on the page gets the strobe back from the row as the page's on would give it; the page is a tap away to turn the strobe off. The timeout counts again from the drag.
- **Let go**: the level is kept and nothing moves. There is no settling motion for a level: it is where the finger left it. The row stays open.
- **Pushing the row shut** goes from the doorway (a tap), from behind the row (a touch), or up from any quick circle.

### Pages from typed settings

A control declares its settings as typed values, and its page is drawn from that list with one kit (map typed.rs):
- **Kinds** (`typed::Kind`): a level (1-255 on the control's own dimmer; the slider is its value, no words), stops (32nds of a stop below the brightest, 0 the brightest and 255 the dimmest: the panel's brightness, its slider dimmest on the left, no words), a share either side of a half (a signed byte of 256ths; its words the share in the person's base), and steps of an octave of a time with an off at the left end (its words "off"/"never" or the time in the person's base). An on and off is a checkbox. A choice of a few (the base, the layout) is the settings' radio group, as it was.
- **The kit is the settings pages' own** (COMPOSITOR.md "Controls"): a typed setting is its name and its value in words (the battery page's fact row), then fluor's slider under it, dragged or tapped, the value under the finger shown as it moves, applied live and kept at the lift, the knob springing to its step's place after the lift (stepped in shown frames). Nothing new for the display to draw: the proposal said rings, and pages draw sliders today (open question 0).
- **Every power-of-two step** (ferros_sys::torch): 32nds of an octave for the strobe, 16ths of an octave for the timeout, 256ths for the share, as brightness is 32nds of a stop; no decimal knob.

### A level's page is a settings page

There is one set of pages, the settings' ring (map `PAGES`); a level's page is the one that holds what it sets, opened a second way. The torch's is a page of its own in the ring (after battery). Brightness's is Appearance, the one page for the look (owner, 2026-11-12: "the more granular screen settings page, which I wanted to combine a few things, brightness, theme, other look related items"): the theme's swatches, brightness (a typed setting since 2026-11-12, the kit's stops), the UI scale, names under circles, and the temporal dither (moved from Developer the same day: the only look setting that lived elsewhere). The name stays Appearance: it is what the page holds. The development views (the hit map, the panel's channels, the release look) stay on Developer, as do the light scope's channel (a sensor's). Swiping round the ring from either goes on to the neighbours as from anywhere. ### The torch page

On (a checkbox: the torch lit at its level, strobe and timeout, or dark; the circle's tap until 2026-11-12), brightness (a level), strobe (off, or a flash every 4 s to 16 a second, read as its period), lit (the share of each flash, 8/256 to 248/256), turns off after (never, or 16 s to about 4 h 33 min). Every value is the torch's own to act on: the strobe and the timeout run on the torch driver's clock (docs/TORCH.md "Settings"), not in the map. Set with the torch dark, they wait for its next light; set while it is lit, it reads them again at once (its op 3). A torch the timeout put out is asked again as the row next shows, so its circle and page read dark.

### Open questions for the owner

Answered 2026-11-12 and gone from this list: the page past the bright end (no drag opens a page; the tap does), brightness's tap (it opens Appearance, the look's one page), and the circles with no page (they are switches: a tap acts, a drag moves the row).

0. **Rings or sliders on the pages.** The proposal said levels as rings like the brightness ring; the settings pages draw fluor's slider, so the torch page and Appearance's brightness do too (one kit, nothing new in the display, no conflict with the quick row's compose). A ring control would be a display op beside the slider and would then serve every page. Rings or sliders?
1. **The strobe's range and safety.** 1/4 Hz to 16 Hz; flashes between about 3 and 30 a second can bring on seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy. Cap it lower, warn on the page, or leave it to the person? And is its value better read as a period ("½ s", as now, in the person's base with the house's doublings) or as flashes a second? A drag on the torch lights the strobe as set, so the row can start a strobe too.
2. **The lit share's range**: 8/256 to 248/256 (at 16 a second the shortest flash is 1.95 ms, past the chip's 1 ms ramp). Right?
3. **The timeout**: 16 s to 2^14 s in 16ths of an octave, counted again from every touch of the torch (a drag, a page change), never by default. Should a default follow the person (an identity setting)?
4. **With an app in front** a drag still sets the level, but a tap on a ring opens nothing (the pages live on the plane). Send the app behind and open the page, as home's tap would?

## Who does what

- **Display** (payloads/display quick.rs): the geometry (`layout`, `doorway_disc`), the sprites (`init`, `render`, `update`) and their composite (`compose`, from `bar_decode`), op 56 (the map's state of the row: the offset in a word, the looks in two more, the console's worker nudged; the reply the slide's length), the ids op 41 reads from the hit map (written at the bars' compose step, hit.rs `bars_row`: 32 + k while the row stands open, its centre and radius from `layout`; 10 the doorway, `doorway_disc`; 11 the top's other ground; 12 behind the row, everything inside the app's circle while any of it shows, so op 27 gives an app no point there; none of these in full screen, hit.rs `row` reading the bars' ids on the helm's rows alone), and the screencap.
- **Map** (payloads/map quick.rs): the finger, the offset, its halving, each ring's drag and every tap, the torch page and the typed settings kit (typed.rs), op 56 sent whenever anything in it moved. Touches reach the map first for the system's places, with an app in front too, as the corners' always have.
- **Kernel**: preferences 39 (sound), 40 (do not disturb, the identity's, taken from the process named fgtw alone), 41 (radios before) and 42-45 (the torch's settings), kept in the device document; and the torch's send right handed to whoever it hands Settings (the map's slot 10, after its use nfc), since init's grants page is full.
- **fgtw** (payloads/fgtw space.rs): op 24, the person's do not disturb into the space and to the kernel; ferros_space kind `DND`.

## Op 56

`quick [offset i32][flags u8][torch level u8][held u8][press radio u8][brightness ring u16 Q16][torch ring u16 Q16][unused u16]`, 15 bytes; the reply `[0][the slide's length u16]`:
- flags bit 0: the torch answers (its level is the next byte); bit 1: a screencap now that the row is hidden.
- held: the quick circle a finger is on (G#FF none). Press radio: the radio circle a finger presses (G#FF none).
- A ring's share G#FFFF: none sent (the display takes brightness from preference 3 and the torch from its level).
