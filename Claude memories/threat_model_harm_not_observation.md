The owner genuinely does not care about being observed ("nothing I do is private... I do not give a flying tuesday about anyone watching me"). The threat model is **intent to harm**, not surveillance. Observation is noise; malice is signal.

**How to apply:** stop framing choices around "privacy/keeping things secret." The reasons to keep things local (e.g. call recordings, transcription) are **sovereignty** (not depending on someone else's infrastructure) and **harm-resistance**, NOT fear of being watched. Don't hedge on "this is sensitive, keep it off the cloud" as if secrecy is the goal — local-first is about control and not being harmable, and most everything is publishable anyway. Consistent with [[public_except_keys_and_handles]] (everything public except keys+handles) and [[ira_is_brand_not_credential]] (defended by fleet-key wraps + lock/shrink, not secrecy). The one hard secret class remains keys + handles; that's about unforgeability/harm-prevention, not modesty.
